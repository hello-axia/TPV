IRAN + DIPLOMACY
Vance flies home without a deal, but Iran says talks aren't dead

The most consequential US-Iran diplomatic engagement of this decade ended Saturday without an agreement. After 21 hours of talks in Islamabad, Vance emerged at a press conference to announce they had not reached a deal. The central sticking point was Iran's refusal to commit to abandoning its nuclear weapons capability. TehranTehranThe capital city of Iran and the seat of its government. When used in news coverage, "Tehran" refers to the Iranian government and its leadership — the same way "Washington" refers to the US government.'s foreign ministry pushed back on Vance's characterization, saying they had agreed on a number of points and that it was natural no final agreement was reached in a single day. Iranian state media accused the US of setting "excessive demands" and said the ball is in America's court. The divergence in how each side described the outcome is significant: the US is framing this as a near-final ultimatum, while Iran is framing it as an ongoing process. What happens to the fragile two-week ceasefire — and whether Vance's "final offer" actually holds — are the central questions hanging over the entire Middle East.

IRAN + MIDDLE EAST
Israel and Lebanon will sit down directly in Washington on Tuesday

Even as the Islamabad talks collapsed, a separate diplomatic track is moving forward: direct talks between Israel and Lebanon are scheduled to begin Tuesday in Washington. Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu confirmed the talks. The core tension: Lebanon's government has been demanding a halt to Israeli strikes, but Israel maintains that Lebanon is not part of the Iran ceasefire framework. Whether these talks yield results will be an early signal of whether broader settlement in the Middle East is actually achievable.
