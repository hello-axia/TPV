Trump fired the two judges who blocked deportations of Öztürk and Mahdawi.

The Trump administration fired immigration judges Roopal Patel and Nina Froes on Friday, along with four colleagues. Patel had ruled in February that there were no grounds to deport Rümeysa Öztürk, a Turkish doctoral student at Tufts who was arrested by ICE after writing an op-ed critical of the university's stance on Palestine. Froes had dismissed deportation proceedings against Mohsen Mahdawi, a Palestinian Columbia student and green card holder who was arrested immediately following his citizenship interview. The Overview

The Most Consequential Fifteen Months in a Generation

Donald Trump returned to the White House on January 20, 2025 with a huge voter base, running on three promises: stopping the border, bringing prices down, and stopping — not starting — any wars. Fifteen months later, the Trump second presidency has been one of extraordinary turbulence and transformation.

The volume of action Trump has undertaken is not in dispute. By the end of his first year, Trump had signed over 221 executive orders. Most presidents average around 100–200 for their entire term. His administration dismantled DEI programs across the federal government, pulled the US out of the World Health Organization and the Paris Climate framework, launched the largest and most public immigration enforcement operation in modern history, and restructured the executive branch. The word most often used by admirers and critics alike is velocity: the sheer speed at which Trump puts his plans into motion.

But velocity is not the same as delivery. The central question: is Donald Trump delivering on the promises he made during his 2024 campaign and January 2025 inauguration? AP News maintains a tight track record of Trump's promises. In this verdict, we will be analyzing what the President has done, what he still has yet to do, and why his approval rating is sitting at −19.

The Disagreement

The People Who Elected Him Are the Ones Asking the Hardest Questions

The standard argument about Trump's second term is the left-right argument. Supporters say he is delivering; critics say he is failing. Both sides are largely talking past each other. The debate is real and widely covered, but it might not be the most important one. The most revealing disagreement is happening inside Trump's own party: among the voters who gave him the second term in the first place.

Trump's 2024 victory was not a standard Republican win. It was a landslide — 312 to 226. Back in 2024, the narrative framed it as the Democrats fundamentally not understanding the working class. Trump gained the votes of working-class whites, Hispanic men who broke toward him in historic numbers, low-income rural communities in swing states, and first-time voters who were drawn to him by his promise of fixing the cost of living. These were not ideological voters, but people looking for real change. Fifteen months later, there is significant change — but not the change many were hoping to see.
